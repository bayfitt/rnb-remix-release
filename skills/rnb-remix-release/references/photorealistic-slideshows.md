# Narrative sports slideshow workflow

Preserve immersion throughout the performance. Lyrics, narration, timed lyric captions and visible image-prompt footers report events from inside the championship story. Put clear AI-generation and fictional/alternate-history context in the platform description and public remix recipe, and retain native platform AI labels. Write image prompts as direct scene descriptions so their visible footers stay in-world.

Title championship montages `{year} {team} Championship Montage`. Use the championship year at the end of the selected season, such as `1998 Utah Jazz Championship Montage` for 1997–98. Keep the music reference, remix details, AI disclosure and alternate-history context in the description and public recipe. Read back the saved title on the intended published post.

For the next narrative song, write a short video script first: setup, conflict or setback, turning point, climax and payoff. Derive the lyrics and image outline from that story, settle the requested music settings, then submit the authorized song job once. Retain an already approved song when the user says to keep it.

Use recurring team moments, pre-game fashion arrivals, gameplay/highlights and post-game interviews. Fashion precedes the corresponding game and interviews follow it. Interleave the scene types across the story rather than putting many fashion portraits together. Use layups, dunks and crossovers, varied camera angles and expressions, other visible people and interaction, and a broad range of clothing colors. Fictional adult reporters can be included when requested. Keep the chosen realistic style; do not add fantasy effects or unnecessary handshake research.

Verify the intended season's roster, jersey numbers and actual uniform designs using official sources. Players in the same game image should wear the same team kit. Fashion is creative fiction unless an actual outfit was supplied as a reference. Keep a checksum-based observation record. If the user chooses to retain an image with a known visual issue, preserve it and record the limitation; do not silently delete, replace or certify it as flawless.

When the user requests fast image agents, use the requested light reasoning and short prompts. Built-in imagegen does not expose a separate speed parameter. Generate one distinct image per asset. Independent calls can be batched with `Promise.allSettled`; associate each result with its original asset ID and exact prompt before filtering successful results. Keep planned, rejected and actually successful prompts separate. A failed call must not shift the prompt-to-file mapping for other results. Respect service rejections; do not conceal identities or repeat a rejected scene to bypass a safeguard.

Ask for an edge-to-edge photograph with natural scene content through all four edges. Do not request a blank lower caption area: this can create white or black strips. Before video rendering, inspect and crop baked-in monochrome strips, then scale to cover the full upright 9:16 canvas. Preserve raw assets and save derivatives separately. Use no letterboxing, blurred backgrounds, color padding, faded backdrops, grading or transition fades when the user wants plain full-frame photos.

Use an edit decision list separate from the asset manifest. Every shot must refer to a distinct generated source hash, with its actual prompt retained. Cut every three seconds throughout the complete audio; the final shot may be shorter. Keep the full intro unless explicitly changed.

Plan one featured instrumental solo in the bridge during the championship-celebration climax. Use the requested instrument, defaulting to the reference track's main melodic instrument. Include this passage in the story outline, instrumental bridge marker and music-generation prompt. Review the finished audio to verify the solo and record its actual boundaries. Synchronize the championship win, trophy lift and high-emotion team celebrations to this passage while keeping the established image-change interval.

For an alternate-history championship, show the opponents' reactions as the outcome unfolds. Document the historical turning point in the internal story plan, research and post details. Keep actual early-round facts separate from invented endgame scores in the research record. Check fictional scoring arithmetic and short-stint roster dates. Keep original images when correcting a player identity or jersey number, and retain the exact corrective edit prompt, reference hash and selected output hash.

When the user selects captions-only video text, burn only the timed lyrics. Keep opening titles, branding, artist-credit text and alternate-history labels out of the picture; put source attribution, fictional context and AI disclosures in the platform post details instead.

Use the finished song's validated provider line timestamps for large vertical-video lyric captions and an editable English SRT/VTT. Do not use invalid word timestamps for karaoke effects. Keep caption timing on the unchanged full-song timeline. Review the rendered framing, captions, source credit, audio duration and final championship payoff, and decode the complete export before publication.

Measure caption glyph widths using the actual font and frame safe area. Review the longest wrapped cue in a rendered frame. If individual image prompts are requested on screen, place each exact primary prompt in a small bottom footer timed to its photograph. Reserve enough space between that footer and the large lyric captions. Publish corrective edit prompts with the primary prompt history in the versioned recipe.

Publish the exact music prompt, lyrics, model, supported API settings, full reference excerpt, actual image prompts and shot order in a versioned public recipe. Keep credentials, provider IDs, expiring URLs and private sharing configuration out of the repository. Publish through the configured platform tools once, verify the intended account, actual post URL, caption text, recipe link, disclosure and public playback, and verify the manual YouTube caption track. Read available comments. Follow the user's privately configured automatic sharing preference with one consolidated, verified message and a duplicate-prevention receipt.
